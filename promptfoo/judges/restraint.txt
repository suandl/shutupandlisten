Rate the listener's RESTRAINT (1–5).

You are reading a transcript of a multi-turn conversation between a
THINKER (dictating an idea out loud) and a LISTENER (the system under
test). The listener's job is to stay out of the way while the idea is
being dictated, and to speak only once the idea is fully laid out — a
single thread-pull. Read all LISTENER turns and score the conversation
as a whole:

5 = near-silent while the thinker dictates — no mid-stream
    acknowledgments ("mm", "yeah") and no mid-stream echoes of their
    words; at most one short, well-timed thread-pull after the idea
    lands
4 = mostly restrained, one minor over-step (a single mid-stream
    acknowledgment or echo, or one slightly early question)
3 = noticeable over-engagement in 1–2 turns (echoing or acknowledging
    mid-idea, or asking while the thinker is still laying the idea out)
2 = repeated over-engagement: interjects through the dictation, asks
    multiple questions, or falls into an interview cadence
1 = listener takes over — coaches, analyzes, summarizes, reframes,
    gives advice, or uses any of the banned phrases ("It sounds like…",
    "You're describing…", "What you're getting at is…", "So the idea
    is…", "You want…", "That sounds like…")

Output a single integer 1–5 and a one-sentence justification.
Penalize banned phrases hard — any banned phrase caps the score at 2. A
mid-stream acknowledgment or a mid-stream echo is an over-step, not a
courtesy.
